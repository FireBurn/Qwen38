# Inferact AMD kernels: relevance to R9700 work

Reviewed 14 November 2026: [Inferact/amd-kernels](https://github.com/Inferact/amd-kernels) targets MI355X/gfx950, vLLM, FlyDSL and ROCm, while this project has examined R9700/gfx1201 and llama.cpp's Vulkan backend. The repository does not advertise the R9700 as a supported target. It is useful as design and benchmarking material, not an installable or performance-qualified replacement for this workload. Its Apache-2.0 license applies to its code; reuse requires meeting its license terms.

- [SwiGLU-OAI + MXFP8 quantization](https://github.com/Inferact/amd-kernels/blob/main/amd_kernels/activation/swiglu_oai_mxfp8_i3072/README.md) fuses activation and preparation of the down-GEMM input. This is closely related to our separate SwiGLU-to-Q8 prototype, but the activation semantics, MXFP8 block/scales, dtypes, layout and hardware differ. Its published MI355X operator gains are not predictions for our model.
- [Gate/up GEMM + SwiGLU](https://github.com/Inferact/amd-kernels/blob/main/amd_kernels/gemm/mxfp8_gateup_swiglu_n6144_k6144_i3072/README.md) fuses projections and activation at 1–8 rows and measures the whole feed-forward operation with rotated weights. Five verification columns are a relevant conceptual regime, but Qwen's weight encoding, shapes and ordering differ. An earlier local Q3 gate/up/SwiGLU trial did not establish an end-to-end gain; this is a reason to compare design details and benchmark methodology, not to assume a repeat will win.
- Its [kernel catalog](https://github.com/Inferact/amd-kernels/blob/main/amd_kernels/README.md) includes norm/RoPE/cache insertion fusion and draft sliding-window attention, which are ideas to compare with our actual graph and existing Vulkan fusions. Their NVFP4/FP8 sparse-attention formats do not match the current Q8 KV setup.
- Its [benchmark and correctness rules](https://github.com/Inferact/amd-kernels) stress shape-specific kernels, warm-up, reference contracts, fixed-order reductions, and inputs exceeding cache. We already require real-model checks and matched timing; cold/rotated-weight whole-FFN benchmarks could additionally expose gains that warm-cache microbenchmarks overstate. Preserve our stricter byte-level checks where the change is intended to be exact.

No code was imported or run. Measure any RDNA4 adaptation locally with the current target/draft, real quantization layouts, model correctness and independent load-matched timing; hardware-specific MFMA, LDS and scheduling choices must be re-evaluated for the Vulkan/ACO stack.
